## 1. Concept and narrative

### 1.1 The concept in two sentences

Flux Atlas is an observatory for the Flux network that behaves like an operating system: a live planet of every node is the desktop, windows open over it like instruments, and the Flux symbol orbits above it as a moon that is the chain itself, taking each block from its producer and firing the payouts down to the three nodes it pays. It is pro-grade and fast before it is playful, it is Flux in colour, type and logo, and it keeps the owner's stache.beer spirit (a boot log, portals, a palette, a terminal, achievements, a screensaver) in how it feels to use.

The one-line version for a pitch: **the network, live, as a place you can open.**

### 1.2 A visit, in five beats

1. **Boot.** The screen is black with a faint hexagon lattice. Real requests run, and as each one finishes a piece of the Flux symbol arrives: the slanted bar as the chain tip syncs, the small hexagon as Cumulus finishes counting (3,378), the big hexagon for Nimbus (1,582), the cap for Stratus (1,764). When the cap lands the four pieces flash white, the symbol is whole, and it lifts off, shrinks along an arc and becomes the moon while a wave of light rolls over the planet from the newest block's producer. Nothing on this screen is theatre: the counts are the real counts, and the log lines are the real stages.
2. **The first block.** About 18 seconds later a block lands. A flare at the producer (Reston), a beam climbs to the moon, the moon's pieces pulse, and the moon pays out in the order the chain lists the payouts, small to large: half a FLUX drifts off its bar toward the dev fund, then beams descend to Taganrog (Cumulus, 1 FLUX), Raleigh (Nimbus, 3.5) and, last and biggest, Helsinki (Stratus, 9). Chips rise: +1.00, +3.50, +9.00. The visitor learns the whole economy of the chain, in under 3 seconds, without reading a word.
3. **Find something.** Control and K. They type `65.109`. The palette lists nodes, a host, a provider. Enter: the camera flies, the marker gains a beacon, a node inspector opens out of the marker with a thin tether line back to it. Seven nodes share this one host and pay one address. Atlas says so, and says what it means.
4. **Understand it.** They open the payment queue (a wheel with a ring per tier and a cursor advancing one slot per block), the operator view (one host, seven nodes, a concentration warning), an app's spec history with diffs back to 2021, the analytics with the reward-cut countdown. Each opens from where it was summoned.
5. **Leave it running.** They press Shift and A, or walk away. The interface dissolves into the planet, the moon grows a little, a clock appears, and every 30 seconds the chain performs the same gesture with whatever nodes it really picked. Touch anything and it all comes back exactly as it was.

### 1.3 Atlas as an operating system: keep, change, cut

The owner's stache.beer is OS-like (boot, portals, terminal, palette, achievements, screensaver, a real mobile layout). Atlas keeps the spirit and removes the costume.

| Idea | Verdict | In Atlas |
|---|---|---|
| Boot screen | Keep, make real | The log is real requests with real numbers. The symbol assembles from them and becomes the moon. Esc skips; a reload in the same session gets 300 ms. |
| Windows through portals | Keep, make physical | The aperture opens each window from its source (dock icon, node marker, result row, the moon) with a tether line to the thing it is about. Windows are routes with URLs. |
| Desktop icons | Change | A dock of launchers, each with a key, a label and a live badge (queue position, alerts). No draggable icons. |
| Command palette | Keep, make it the front door | One box understands heights, hashes, transaction ids, addresses, IPs, collateral outpoints, app names, places, providers, versions and commands. |
| Terminal | Keep, make it a navigator | The same verbs as the UI (`node`, `app`, `goto`, `queue`, `moon`), autocomplete, history, streaming commands (`tail blocks`). |
| Achievements | Keep, quieten | 24, local only, no emoji, never blocking, at most three toasts per session. They teach features. |
| Screensaver | Keep, drive it with events | Ambient mode is a director that follows real events. The moon is the only chrome that stays. |
| Mobile layout | Keep, redesign | Its own layout: the globe as stage, one bottom sheet, five tabs. Not a squeezed desktop. |
| Fake file system, fake apps, skeuomorphic chrome | Cut | Nothing in Atlas pretends to be something it is not. |
| Looping decoration, custom cursors, idle particles | Cut | Every motion corresponds to an event, or to the clock. |
| Sound | Change | Off by default. Optional generative sound exists in ambient mode only. |
| Retro gimmicks (CRT curvature, glitch text) | Cut | Scanlines exist once, as the archive grade on the globe, because they mean "this is the past". |

### 1.4 Principles

1. **The globe is home, windows are guests.** One planet, mounted once, alive from boot to ambient. Windows float over it, never replace it.
2. **Live is the truth.** Every number ticks, every time is a counting "seconds ago", every animation is an event that really happened. If the feed stops the interface says so in words.
3. **The chain has a place.** The moon is the chain. It is where blocks go and where money comes from. You can point at it, hover it, open it.
4. **Flux first.** Colour, type and logo come from the Flux brand. Nothing decorative overrides them, and nothing that belongs to the product (tiers, status, charts) is ever allowed to touch the logo.
5. **One loud moment per 30 seconds.** The Beat performs. Everything else is quiet: information arrives, it does not dance.
6. **Honest data.** Pending is never shown as confirmed, unknown is never shown as zero, estimates are labelled, sample values in the mock are labelled illustrative.
7. **Everything is a link.** A node, an app, a block, a transaction, an address, a country, a provider, a version: each has a URL, a hover card and a palette entry.
8. **Dense but legible.** Pro-grade density on desktop (13 px UI, tabular figures, no decoration between numbers) with a clear type ramp and generous targets on touch.
9. **Keyboard and phone are first-class.** Every dock item has a key. The phone layout is designed, not derived.
10. **Personality lives in the margins.** The boot log, the terminal, empty states, achievements and the ambient sentence may be dry and a little playful. Data surfaces never are.

### 1.5 Voice and tone

Sentence case everywhere. No ALL CAPS labels (the one exception is the tiny status badge, set in Montserrat with `--tracking-caps`), no tracked-out eyebrows above headings, no decorative middle-dot chains. Copy says what is true, in plain verbs, and stays short: "Stratus node in Falkenstein, paid 1,654 blocks ago". Errors say what happened and what to do, in the interface's voice, and never apologise.

| Surface | Voice | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Data surfaces | factual, terse | "Next payment in 14.7 h. Queue position 1 of 1,764." |
| Live feed | past tense, one line, subject first | "Node joined, Stratus in Falkenstein" |
| Boot log | dry, present tense | "Load nodes   3,378 of 6,724" |
| Terminal | dry, a little playful | "7 nodes on host 65.109.26.93, one operator. Not a great place for all your eggs." |
| Empty states | direct an action | "No nodes watched yet. Select a node and press Shift and W to watch it." |
| Errors | what happened, what next | "Could not reach the live stream. Showing data from 14:02:11. Retrying in 3 s." |
| Achievements | wry, short | "First contact. You opened the moon." |
| About Flux | plain, warm, the brand's own mission line in Lora | "To build a scalable, decentralized network of computing power for the people, by the people." |

Words we use: node, host (one IP), site (a city's worth of hosts), operator (an address that owns nodes), block, payout, tier, queue. Words we avoid: miner (the producer earns nothing extra in PoN), validator (not a Flux term), "sync" for the UI's own refresh.

## 2. Information architecture

### 2.1 The model: one planet, many windows

Atlas has four layers, always in this order from back to front (z tokens in parentheses):

1. **The globe** (`--z-globe`): one canvas, mounted once, never unmounted. It is the desktop. Its state (camera, layers, selection, time) is part of the URL.
2. **Shell chrome** (`--z-globe-hud` to `--z-statusbar`): the top bar, the dock, the Pulse feed, the aim strip, the block rail with its timeline strip, the status bar, the moon's hit target. These are persistent instruments.
3. **Windows** (`--z-window` to `--z-window-max`): inspectors and tools. A window is a route: it has a URL, a title, a tether (when it is about something on the globe) and a state that survives reload.
4. **Overlays**: menus, toasts, the command palette, tooltips, the ambient layer, the boot screen, grain.

The moon belongs to layer 1 (it is drawn by the globe renderer) and to layer 2 (its hit target and tooltip are DOM, so it is reachable by keyboard and assistive tech). It is the only object in the product that is both part of the world and a control.

### 2.2 Routes and deep links

Every route is shareable. The path names the focused window, the query carries the rest of the state. Canonical forms are given; aliases redirect with `replaceState`.

| Route | Opens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `/` | the bare globe | Pulse, rail and aim strip visible, no window. The mock's default screenshot is the deep link `/node/65.109.26.93:16147`. |
| `/node/:key` | node inspector | Canonical key `ip:port` (`65.109.26.93:16147`). Also accepts a collateral outpoint `txid:n` and a node id. Flies the camera to the node. |
| `/host/:ip` | node inspector on the Host tab | Lists the up to 8 nodes on one IP (ports 16127 to 16197), their tiers and ranks. |
| `/app/:name` | app inspector | `/app/BitcoinWhitepaper`. Constellation on the globe. `/app/:name/history/:n` deep-links a spec version and its diff. |
| `/block/:key` | explorer, block view | Height or hash. Prev and next arrows. |
| `/tx/:txid` | explorer, transaction view | Flow diagram, inputs and outputs, raw. |
| `/address/:addr` | explorer, address view | Balance, history, UTXOs, and the nodes this address owns or is paid for (links to `/operator/:addr`). |
| `/mempool`, `/supply`, `/richlist` | explorer extras | Mempool as a ring of confirming nodes, supply and emission curve, rich list with node context. |
| `/queue`, `/queue/:tier` | payment queue wheel | `:tier` is `cumulus`, `nimbus` or `stratus` and isolates one ring. |
| `/analytics/:tab?` | network analytics | Tabs: `overview`, `geography`, `hosting`, `capacity`, `versions`, `churn`, `archaeology`. |
| `/operator/:addr` | operator view | Several addresses: `?addr=a,b`. Stored locally, shareable by URL. |
| `/time` | time machine | The timeline strip expands. `?t=2026-09-26T08:00Z&speed=60`. |
| `/weather` | network weather | The weather layer plus a verdict chip. No window. |
| `/terminal` | terminal window | `?cmd=` prefills but never auto-runs. |
| `/ambient` | ambient kiosk | No chrome, auto-reconnect, wake-lock. Any input exits to `/`. |
| `/about` | About Flux (the moon) | Live network totals, the symbol's anatomy, the mission, links. Opened by clicking the moon, key M, palette, dock. |
| `/settings` | settings | Motion, performance tier, units, notifications, keyboard. |
| `/q/:text` | palette result page | Resolves an ambiguous string (64 hex, a bare number) with the omnibox algorithm, then redirects to the real route. |

**Extra windows.** The path is the focused window. Other open windows ride in `?w=` in stack order, at most two: `/node/65.109.26.93:16147?w=queue,app:BitcoinWhitepaper`.

### 2.4 Window behaviour

- **Open**: through the aperture (6.4 A), from the source that summoned it. If the window type is already open it is retargeted instead (node to node swaps content, retargets the tether).
- **Close**: the close control, Esc (selection clears first, then the topmost window), or opening the bare globe with G. Close reverses the aperture.
- **Dock or float**: the pin control. Docked windows reserve the right inset so the globe re-centres in the free area (300 ms); floating windows overlay it.
- **Drag and snap**: drag by the title bar. Within 24 px of the left or right edge a drop zone appears; release snaps with a spring. Neighbours re-tile.
- **Resize**: inspector width 360 to 640 by its left edge; floating windows by edges and corners down to `--window-min-w` by `--window-min-h`.
- **Minimise**: collapses to a dot under its dock launcher; the launcher shows it. Restore is a click on the launcher.
- **Pop out**: opens `?popout=1` in a new browser window for a second monitor; the pop-out shares state through BroadcastChannel and keeps its own tether-less chrome.
- **Focus**: the focused window has the accent rim and the moving highlight; content never dims when unfocused. Raising a window never changes its position.
- **Memory**: the last placement per window type is remembered per browser, and restored on the next open.
- **Reload**: the full state (route, `?w=`, camera, layers, time) restores from the URL; there is no separate session store to get out of sync.

### 2.5 The omnibox

The omnibox is the top-bar field and the command palette (Control or Command and K, or click). It is one input with typed results. It resolves locally first, then asks the server for the rest; the resolution order follows `docs/research/explorer-api.md` section 4.

| Input class | Pattern | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Block height | digits, at most the tip; `#2996929` and `2,996,929` are accepted | Block `2,996,929` |
| 64 hex | a block hash, a transaction id or a collateral transaction id | one typed hit per real match, resolved in parallel |
| Transparent address | `t1` or `t3` and 33 base58 characters, validated locally | Address, with "owns N nodes" when known |
| Shielded address | `zs1...` or `zc...` | an explanation: shielded addresses are private and not indexed |
| Node IP | IPv4 with optional port, IPv6 in brackets | Node, or Host when the port is omitted |
| Collateral outpoint | `txid:n` or `COutPoint(txid, n)` | Node |
| App name | 3 to 63 letters and digits | App, with fuzzy suggestions |
| Place | a city, country or continent | Go to (fly and filter) |
| Provider or ASN | `hetzner`, `AS24940` | Provider, with its node share |
| Version | `8.20.0` | Version: share and stragglers |
| Command | words that match a verb: `ambient`, `queue`, `weather`, `moon`, `about flux` | an action row with its key |

Results group by kind (Nodes, Hosts and providers, Apps, Blocks, Transactions, Addresses, Go to, Commands), each row carrying a tier glyph or icon, a bold matched prefix, a sub-line and meta (status chip, key). Enter opens the best hit; Tab cycles the kind filter; Shift and Enter opens in a new window; Alt and Enter only flies the camera. With an empty query the palette shows recent items and the next payees.

Examples with real fixtures: `65.109` lists the nodes on Helsinki's `65.109.x.x` range and the host `65.109.26.93` (7 nodes, one operator); `BitcoinWhitepaper` opens the app; `t1cz5PE2Q` (the start of the operator address) opens the operator view; `8aa97365b148e212...` opens the transaction in block 2,996,907; `moon` and `about flux` open About Flux.

### 2.6 URL state

| Query | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `c=lat,lon,size` | camera (one decimal; `size` in percent of viewport height). Written with `replaceState`, at most once per 500 ms of idleness. |
| `l=nodes,mesh.sel,-labels` | layers on or off. `mesh` has modes `off`, `sel`, `flow`. |
| `tier`, `cc`, `org`, `asn`, `ver`, `arcane`, `hosting`, `watched` | filters; they dim the globe and apply to tables |
| `sel=ip:port,...` | selected nodes (at most 50) |
| `t`, `speed` | time machine instant (UTC) and playback speed |
| `w` | extra windows (see 2.2) |
| `addr` | operator addresses |
| `q` | palette text |
| `popout=1` | pop-out chrome |

Selection, windows and time use `pushState`, so Back and Forward walk the user's actual story; the camera never adds history entries. Back and Forward restore the camera with a shortened 600 ms flight.

### 2.7 Menus and the dock

The top bar keeps three menus (View, Go, Window) for discoverability; every item shows its key. The dock (a 56 px pill at the left) holds the launchers in this order: Globe, Nodes, Apps, Explorer, Payment queue, Analytics, Time machine, Operator, Terminal, then a divider and Weather layer, Ambient mode, Settings. About Flux is reached through the moon, the palette, the key M and, on desktop, as a dock item at the very end (the white Flux symbol mark, the only launcher that is the brand rather than a line icon). The full keyboard map is in 10.4.

## 4. The live-first system

Atlas exists to show the network as it changes. This section says what the user sees, where, and what each live instrument means; section 6 says how it moves (and holds the rate-limit, hover-freeze and reduced-motion rules), section 7 says how the globe draws it.

### 4.1 One clock, many instruments

Every live surface runs off one event clock: the chain's 30 second cadence, measured from each block's own header time and corrected by the server's observed latency. Nothing keeps its own timer, so the tip, the countdown, the rail's ghost card, the aim strip and the moon can never disagree.

| Instrument | Shows | Driven by | Update |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The moon's ring** | a hexagonal hairline around the moon filling 0 to 1 across the block interval; the chain's clock made visible | `--beat` | continuous, steps once per second under reduced motion |
| Beat chip (top bar) | a 28 px ring, the tip height, "next in N s" | `--beat`, tip | ring continuous, text once per second |
| Top-bar light | a small white light travelling the bar's lower edge at `--beat` of its width | `--beat` | continuous |
| Rail ghost card | the next height, the countdown, "20 tx, 4.0 KB waiting" (the mempool), a fill that advances | tip, mempool | once per second |
| Aim strip | "Next payout in 18 s" and the three next payees | next payees, tip | once per second |
| Status bar | "Live, 41 ms", "tip 2 s" and the other freshness chips | connection, ingest ages | once per second |
| Pulse | rows with counting ages ("12 s ago") | event timestamps | once per second, never per row |
| Ambient captions | the clock, the last block's age, the next payee sentence | all of the above | once per second |

The ring and the chip mirror each other on purpose: the moon is the chain, and the chip is the same gauge in a place a keyboard user and a phone user can always reach.

**Late blocks.** Flux intervals vary. At 35 s without a block the chip and the status bar read "block late 41 s" in `--status-warn`; at 90 s they turn `--status-crit` and the copy separates "chain quiet" from "feed lost" using the connection state. The moon never takes a status colour (5.1, 7.10.11): it dims its glow from 0.55 to 0.25 and stops its ring, and the warning lives in the chip beside it.

### 4.2 The Beat, read as chain semantics

The choreography (6.4 I) is not decoration: each step is something the chain did. That is why the moon can carry it.

| What you see | What happened | Field |
|---|---|---|
| A flare at one node, then a wave rolling over the planet | a node produced block H. Its collateral is in the block header; in Proof of Node the producer earns no extra subsidy, it signs | block `collateral`, `blocksig` |
| A beam climbing from the producer to the moon | the block was submitted to the chain | `block` message |
| The moon's pieces pulsing together | the chain accepted it (the four pieces are the four outputs of the coinbase) | coinbase with exactly four outputs |
| The bar flashing first, and "+0.50 dev fund" drifting away | the first output of the coinbase, 0.5 FLUX plus the block's fees, goes to the dev fund address and has no node to point at | coinbase output 0 |
| Three beams descending in the coinbase's order, small to large (Cumulus, Nimbus, Stratus) | the three paid nodes: 1, 3.5 and 9 FLUX | coinbase outputs 1 to 3 matched to the node list |
| Chips "+1.00", "+3.50", "+9.00" at the payees | amounts landed | `payouts` |

**Why this order.** It is the order in which the chain lists the outputs (output 0 the dev fund, then Cumulus, Nimbus, Stratus), so the animation is a true reading of the transaction; it matches the tier capsules (one, two, three); and it builds to the largest payout instead of spending it first, so the last beam to leave, and the last to land, is the 9 FLUX payout. Lists and summaries keep reading order, largest first (the aim strip, the terminal, the ambient sentence, the About legend); only the relay performs in coinbase order.

Payees are known one block ahead (`fluxnodecurrentwinner`), which is why the aim reticles exist (4.3): the beams end on marks that were already there.

### 4.3 Pre-aim and the payment queue

**Pre-aim.** Within 3 s of each landing (2.6 s in the mock, once the last beam has landed) the three winners for the next block resolve and Atlas aims: a reticle on each payee on the globe (6.4 M, 7.17), three chips in the aim strip ("Helsinki 9.0", "Raleigh 3.5", "Taganrog 1.0"), the same three on the rail's ghost card, and an inspector line on any selected or watched node that is one of them: "Paid in the next block". A watched payee's chip is ringed in white.

**The payment queue wheel** (`/queue`, dock Q, the mock's `shots/desktop-queue.webp`). One node per tier is paid in every block, in queue order, so each tier is a ring and the cursor is the next payee.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Rings | outside in: Cumulus (3,378 nodes), Nimbus (1,582), Stratus (1,764); 22 px thick, 120 ticks each; the front of each queue (the next 6.5 percent) drawn as a bright comet arc approaching the cursor |
| Cursor | a white marker at 12 o'clock with a dashed leader to the centre |
| Centre | "Next payout in 18 s" and the next height (2,996,930) |
| Your nodes | markers on the rings at angle `360 - rank / total x 360` (clockwise from the top), ringed in white, rank 0 larger |
| Side panel | per tier: tier glyph, name, node count, reward, cycle length (Cumulus 28 h, Nimbus 13.2 h, Stratus 14.7 h), then "Your nodes" with rank and ETA |
| Interaction | hover a slot for node, rank and ETA; click opens the inspector; search in the palette highlights its node; `/queue/stratus` isolates a ring |
| On landing | each ring advances one slot over 600 ms (`--ease-out-expo`) and the paid node's marker jumps to the back of its ring (specified, not mocked) |

ETA text: "Next block" at rank 0; seconds under 90 s; minutes under an hour; hours above ("in 14.7 h"). New nodes join at the back.

### 4.4 Freshness, connection and honesty

The tables live in 6.5 (freshness states fresh, aging, stale, dead; connection states Live, Syncing, Reconnecting, Offline, Upstream stale). Where they appear:

- **Status bar, left**: the connection chip ("Live, WebSocket, 41 ms"), then one freshness chip per ingest path (tip 2 s, nodes 4 s, apps 41 s, stats 7 min, mesh 12 min).
- **Window title bars**: each window shows the freshness of its own data in its title bar (an inspector shows "nodes 4 s", analytics "stats 7 min").
- **Stale looks the same everywhere**: the value keeps its last number, dims to `--text-3`, gains "as of 14:02:11", and its chip turns `--status-warn` with the word "stale". Nothing is ever blanked to zero.

Honest-data rules: pending is never shown as confirmed (a pending app update has its own state and expiry); unknown is never shown as zero (an explicit "Unknown" chip, never a blank or a 0); estimates are labelled ("about $6.65", "sample values" in the mock); a fixture value that is illustrative is marked as such where it is shown.

### 4.5 Lifecycles

**Node lifecycle** (a horizontal stepper with a live cursor, used in the node inspector):

| Stage | Meaning | Looks |
|---|---|---|
| Started | a start transaction is in; unconfirmed (start transactions expire after 240 blocks) | hollow marker, `--status-pending` |
| Joined | first confirmation | filled, `--status-ok` |
| Heartbeat | re-confirmation about every 4.2 h (500 blocks) | live cursor |
| At risk | 560 or more blocks since the last check-in | `--status-warn`, hollow ring on the globe (`--globe-risk`) |
| Expired | 640 blocks without a check-in (about 5.3 h) | `--status-crit`, fades out on the globe |

Under it, a **check-in gauge** (0 to 640 blocks; zones healthy to 500, due 500 to 560, at risk 560 to 640) ticks with every block, and a 90-cell uptime strip shows a cell per day. DoS bans (720 blocks) appear as a crit chip.

**App lifecycle** (same stepper component):

| Stage | Meaning | Looks |
|---|---|---|
| Pending | the register or update message is seen but not mined; the median wait is about 168 s and about 15 percent are never mined. A live 1 h expiry counts down; "Not mined" is a muted end state, never a disappearance | `--status-pending`, ghost markers on the globe where it may land |
| Confirmed | mined and paid (FLUX amount shown) | `--status-ok` |
| Installing | n of m instances installing | spinning arc on each instance (`--globe-install`) |
| Running | instances spawned | constellation draws |

**Expiry.** An app's expiry is in blocks (the BitcoinWhitepaper fixture: 88,000 blocks from its last update, about 30.5 days at 30 s). The app inspector shows a ring of the fraction remaining, the date, and the block. Renewals repeat the same spec and are grouped in the history.

**Spec archaeology.** The app History tab and the Analytics Archaeology tab read the permanent messages (about 70,000 over six years): register, renew (grouped: "Renewed 4 times, same spec"), update with a readable diff of the real changes, versions 2 to 8, FLUX paid per message. Enterprise (encrypted) apps show only their public fields and say why ("Compose is encrypted. 906 of 1,573 v8 apps are").

### 4.6 The emission countdown

The first 10 percent reward cut lands at block 3,071,200. At the demo clock that is 74,271 blocks away, about 25 days 18 hours, around 2026-10-26; the cut takes 14 FLUX to 12.6 (Stratus 9.0 to 8.1, Nimbus 3.5 to 3.15, Cumulus 1.0 to 0.9, dev fund 0.5 to 0.45) and repeats every 1,051,200 blocks. It appears in four places: a chip in the status bar ("Reward cut in 25d 18h"), the Analytics cut card (blocks, days, progress from the start of Proof of Node at block 2,020,000, currently 92.9 percent, and the before and after per tier), the Explorer home, and About Flux. The ceremony itself is 6.4 O.

#### 5.1.6 Misuse

The book's six misuses apply to every Atlas surface: (1) do not change the colour; (2) do not change the typeface of the wordmark; (3) do not change the colour of the symbol and wordmark to anything outside the guides (the book shows red and green); (4) do not change the proportions; (5) do not put a border on the logo; (6) do not place it on a colour that blends with it (for example Blue Wave on a mid blue). Atlas adds these, because it has a product palette the book does not:

- No tier colour, status colour, chart colour or accent tint other than the brand shades ever touches the symbol, the moon or the wordmark.
- No outer glow, drop shadow, gradient fill or blend mode on the flat logo. (The moon is the one sanctioned lighting of the symbol; it stays inside the brand colours, 7.10.11.)
- No animation of the flat logo's colours. It may fade, scale and translate; it never pulses through a hue.
- No coloured chip, badge or ring overlapping it. A tier colour sits beside a piece, never on one.
- The wordmark is the official artwork, never retyped in another face.

#### 5.1.7 Pattern and imagery

The book's pattern pages show a hexagon lattice and a staggered parallelogram pattern in tones of Blue Wave. Atlas uses the **hexagon lattice**, redrawn pointy-top to match the symbol (`--pattern-hex`, tile `--pattern-hex-size`, a 1 px hairline in `#86A1DA` at 16 percent). It appears on the boot screen, the About Flux header band, empty states and the phone splash, always under a radial mask so it fades out before any edge. It is never used behind dense data. The parallelogram is the symbol's bar; it appears only as that piece.

Imagery: no stock photography and no decorative 3D renders. The image is the globe: a dot-matrix planet in tones of Blue Wave, lit by one sun, carrying real events. The book's "clean geometric shapes in blue" guidance is met by the dot matrix, the hexagon lattice and the moon.

### 5.2 Colour system

**Design intent.** A black world lit by one sun and one brand. Flux blue is the structure of the world: the chain, the UI, focus, the planet itself. White is attention: the colour of light that is about to matter. Three emissive tier colours are the inhabitants, and they arrive only where a payout arrives. Status colours are reserved for state.

**Rules**

1. Flux blue (`--accent-*`) is the chain and the UI structure: explorer surfaces, focus rings, selection, links, primary buttons, the shockwave, the planet's dots and atmosphere.
2. White (`--hot`, `--hot-2`) is attention: selection rims, block flares, beam heads, the lit edge of every window, the moon's faces. It is never a large fill.
3. Tier colours are reserved for tiers. They never appear as "series 2" of a non-tier chart, never on the logo or the moon, and tier is never colour-only (the tier glyph is a 1/2/3 capsule meter and globe markers differ in size and rings).
4. Status colours (`--status-*`) are reserved and ship with an icon and a word, never colour alone.
5. Text never wears a data colour. Marks carry series colours; labels use the text ramp.
6. One light source. The sun sits top-left in the UI, always: window rims are white along the top, the globe's sun-facing limb is lit, the night side is cool. The real terminator follows UTC, but UI light never moves.
7. Dark only. There are no light surfaces; white appears as text, rims, beam heads and the logo. The deepest colour is Flux Black (`--void`), the brightest is Flux White (`--hot`).
8. Brand precedence. Where a product colour would sit on or against the logo or the moon, the brand colour wins and the product colour moves beside it.

Chamfered shapes use `mask` (keeps border radius) or `clip-path`, and `filter: drop-shadow` for shadow because `box-shadow` ignores masks. Chamfers are a signature, not a pattern: never on list rows, inputs, chips or tooltips.

### 5.6 Data-visualisation palettes (validated)

**Mark specs.** Lines 2 px with round joins; markers 8 px or larger with a 2 px surface ring; bars at most 24 px thick with a 4 px rounded data end, square at the baseline; areas at 10 to 12 percent of the series hue (`--viz-area-alpha`); hairline solid grid `--viz-grid`; a 2 px surface gap between touching marks. **Figures.** Stat tile contract: label (sentence case, no colon), value (Montserrat 600, tabular, auto-compact), optional delta (signed, against a named period, arrow plus sign plus colour), optional 12-point sparkline in `--viz-1` with its end dot ringed in the surface colour. Exactly one hero figure per view.

**Number formats**: nodes `6,724`; heights `2,996,914`; FLUX 2 decimals in summaries (`9.00 FLUX`), 8 in transaction detail; hashes middle-truncated; percentages 1 decimal; bytes `3.4 KB`; bandwidth `949 Mbps`; durations `5d 3h`; times `19:39:04 UTC` absolute with a relative companion (`12 s ago`) that counts up live.


## 6. Motion system

Atlas is live-first: the network changes every second and the interface must show it without turning into a slot machine. The motion system has one loud moment (the Beat, every 30 s, performed by the moon as a relay from producer to payees), one quiet perpetual motion (the moon's orbit), a quiet vocabulary for everything else, and hard rules for bursts. All timing values are tokens (`--dur-*`, `--ease-*`, `--stagger`). The moon's own geometry is in 7.10; this section says when and how things move.

### 6.1 Principles

1. **Camera leads, UI follows.** When an action targets something on the globe, the camera starts moving first; the window arrives when the target is about 65% of the way there.
2. **One loud motion at a time.** The attention budget allows a single loud effect in view (the block relay, a window aperture, a camera fly). Quiet motion (tickers, sparkles, freshness counts) may coexist with it. Loud effects queue; they never stack.
3. **Motion explains cause.** Things come from where they belong: windows open out of the node, dock icon, result or moon that summoned them; blocks enter from the Beat edge; money travels producer, then moon, then payee, in the order the chain paid it.
4. **Live is calm.** Information arrives, it does not perform. Only the Beat performs.
5. **Never move a target under the pointer.** Live lists freeze while hovered or focused and queue new items behind a "N new" pill. The moon slows to 15 percent speed under the pointer for the same reason.
6. **Everything is interruptible.** Input never waits for an animation, and pointer events are never blocked. Any animation resolves to its end state instantly when interrupted.
7. **Compositor only.** Animate `transform`, `opacity`, `clip-path`/`mask` and small `filter`s. Layout properties are off limits, except `grid-template-rows` for disclosure. Lists use FLIP.
8. **Budgets.** Input to visible response in 100 ms or less. 60 fps on the globe at 1440p. Motion is never the only carrier of information.
9. **One thing orbits.** The moon is the only object that moves without an event, at one lap per four minutes. Everything else is still until something happens.

### 6.3 Choreography rules

- **Order**: globe or camera, then container, then content, then detail. Each step starts when the previous one is 35 to 45% complete.
- **Stagger**: 28 ms per item, at most 8 items (224 ms); the rest arrive with the last.
- **Origins**: transforms are anchored to the source (icon, node, result row, the moon), never the window centre.
- **Scale**: windows enter from 0.94, toasts from 0.98, nothing scales from zero except the aperture (clip-path).
- **Hierarchy of attention**: the Beat (producer, moon and payees read as one gesture), then user-initiated motion, then my-node alerts, then network events, then ambient sparkle. A higher class pauses the lower for its duration (sparkle stops sampling during a relay).
- **White is the attention colour.** Flares, beam heads, selection rims and the first pixel of any new thing are white (`--hot`); the body of a beam is Blue Wave going up to the moon and the payee's tier colour coming down. Nothing on the moon is ever tinted (5.1.8).
- **Pointer feedback** is under 100 ms and never gated: hover rings, press scale 0.98, focus rings.

**A. Window open through the aperture** (the owner's portal idea, rebuilt for Atlas). Source point S is the dock icon, the clicked node's screen position, the palette row, or the moon's centre.

**B. Snap and dock.** Dragging within 24 px of a snap edge fades a drop zone in (blue wash 12%, 1 px dashed rim, 140 ms). On release the window springs to the zone (`--ease-spring-snappy`, 340 ms). Neighbours re-tile with FLIP. The globe's inset animates 300 ms `--ease-in-out` so the globe and the moon stay centred in the free area.

**C. Focus.** Focus arrival flares the rim once (white to the window accent over 900 ms). The focused rim sweeps a highlight along the top edge every 9 s (`--sweep`). Unfocused windows lose the sweep and the glow; content never dims.

**D. Number ticker (odometer).** Detect on the formatted string. Only changed digits animate, from the last digit backward, 40 ms apart. A digit rolls on a vertical strip: up when the value rises, down when it falls, `translateY(+-100%)` over `--dur-tick` with `--ease-out-expo`. Width is stable: Montserrat is proportional by default, so every ticking Montserrat numeral carries `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` (Plex Mono and Open Sans already are). A direction tint (`--div-pos` or `--div-neg`) holds 600 ms then returns to `--text-1`. Changes smaller than 0.05% of the value swap silently. Big jumps (initial load, more than 5% in one update, time-machine scrub) count up with `easeOutExpo` over 900 ms from the previous value, or swap instantly during scrubbing. Seconds counters ("12 s ago") swap instantly, never roll.

**E. Sparkline live draw.** A new point reveals with a clip from the last x to the new x over 420 ms, the end dot rides the line, the y domain eases over 300 ms, and the oldest point leaves by a one-point shift. No redraw flash.

**I. The Beat: a block lands, and the moon relays it.** This is the one scheduled performance. A `block` message arrives over the WebSocket carrying height, hash, producer and payouts. The moon is the chain: the producer hands it the block, it accepts it, and it pays. That is small to large, a crescendo, and it is also the order of the tier capsules, one, two, three. The last beam to leave and the last to land is the 9 FLUX Stratus payout, and the payment toast follows it. One constant, `DOWNLINK_ORDER`, drives the flashes, the beams, the chips and the toast:

The same timeline as data, for the choreographer (event names are the ones the frontend emits; `DOWNLINK_ORDER` is the single source of order, and everything else derives from it):

```ts
export const DOWNLINK_ORDER = ['dev', 'cumulus', 'nimbus', 'stratus'] as const;   // coinbase outputs 0 to 3
const PIECE = { dev: 'bar', cumulus: 'smallHex', nimbus: 'bigHex', stratus: 'cap' } as const;
const T_UP = 60, D_UP = 720, T_RECV = T_UP + D_UP /* 780 */, T_FIRE = T_RECV + 110 /* 890 */, GAP = 130, D_DOWN = 900;

const relay = (ev: BlockEvent): Step[] => [
  { at: 0,      kind: 'flare',     node: ev.producer, dur: 900 },
  { at: 0,      kind: 'shock',     from: ev.producer, dur: 1700, reach: 62 },   // + a second ring at +400 ms, 2100 ms, 88 degrees, when ev.emission
  { at: T_UP,   kind: 'uplink',    node: ev.producer, dur: D_UP },
  { at: T_RECV, kind: 'moonFlare', piece: 'all', dur: 900 },                     // the moon receives
  ...DOWNLINK_ORDER.flatMap((slot, i): Step[] => {
    const t = T_FIRE + i * GAP;                                                  // 890, 1020, 1150, 1280
    if (slot === 'dev') return [
      { at: t - 60, kind: 'moonFlare', piece: 'bar', dur: 340 },                 // the bar flashes first
      { at: t + 60, kind: 'fundChip',  text: '+0.50 dev fund', dur: 2300 },      // no beam: the dev fund has no node
    ];
    const payee = ev.payees[slot];
    return [
      { at: t - 60,            kind: 'moonFlare',    piece: PIECE[slot], dur: 340 },
      { at: t,                 kind: 'downlink',     tier: slot, node: payee.node, dur: D_DOWN },
      { at: t + D_DOWN - 40,   kind: 'payoutLanded', tier: slot, node: payee.node, amount: payee.amount },   // 1880, 2010, 2140
    ];
  }),
  { at: 2600, kind: 'aimNext' },                                                  // the next payees resolve
];
// Reduced motion: flare (static), uplink 380 ms, receive at 440, every downlink at 480 together, landed at 860; no shock, no fund chip travel.
```

If a second block arrives within 3 s, it plays the compact version (uplink and downlinks only, 1.2 s). The Beat is never skipped and never delayed more than one frame. Between landings the moon's ring advances continuously (a hexagon stroke filling clockwise from the top vertex, a white dot at its head, steps once per second under reduced motion); in the last 3 s it brightens by 20% (anticipation). If 35 s pass without a block the chip and status bar read "block late 41 s" in amber, and after 90 s in critical with "chain quiet" told apart from "feed lost" by the WebSocket state; the moon dims its glow from 0.55 to 0.25 and stops its ring but keeps orbiting and never takes the status colour (4.1, 7.10.11).

**J. Boot: the symbol assembles, then lifts off and becomes the moon.** The log lines are real stages; progress is the weighted completion of real requests and never regresses. The four pieces of the Flux symbol arrive as their data does and are drawn by the **globe's own renderer in the globe's own canvas**, so when the symbol lifts off it simply becomes the moon: same canvas, same paths, no hand-off seam. **N. Lifecycle steppers (node and app).** Both lifecycles are horizontal steppers with a live cursor, reusing one component.

### 6.5 Live feed rules

These are the rules that keep a live interface readable. They apply to the Pulse feed, the block rail, toasts, tables, counters and the globe.

**Event classes**

| Class | Examples | Globe | Feed | Toast | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 Beat | block landed | full relay (6.4 I) | pinned block row | never | one per block, never dropped; second within 3 s plays compact |
| P1 Mine | payment to a watched or operator node, watched node status change, alert rules | ring, beam, persistent marker | highlighted row | yes, one at a time, queue up to 3 | merged per node within 10 s |
| P2 Network | node joined, left or changed status; app deployed, updated, expired; version rollout | birth, implode or pulse; constellation flash | yes | milestones only ("50 nodes joined in the last hour") | globe 8 effects per second, feed 4 visible rows per second |
| P3 Ambient | heartbeats (fluxnode confirm transactions), mempool transactions, peer updates | micro sparkle, at most 12 per second, sampled | one aggregated row per block ("Confirmed 14 nodes") | never | sampled, counts only |

**Bursts.** In any 1 s window, the first 3 P2 events render as individual rows; the rest collapse into one summary row ("+27 more: nodes confirmed") that updates in place (its count ticks) while the burst continues, freezes after 2 s of quiet, and expands on click or Enter. The globe budget is 200 arcs and 50 concurrent pulses; overflow drops the lowest class first, oldest first, and dropped events are still counted in the feed. The feed keeps 200 rows in the DOM (virtualised) and 3,000 in memory.

**Hover-freeze.** While the pointer is over a live list, or keyboard focus is inside it, insertions queue. A "3 new" pill appears at the list edge (120 ms); clicking it, or leaving the list for 600 ms, flushes the batch with one FLIP. The block rail freezes the same way when scrolled into history, with a "Jump to live" pill; auto-follow resumes at the live edge.

**Freshness.** Every live surface carries a freshness chip derived from its source cadence (tip 3 to 5 s, nodes 60 to 120 s, apps 60 to 120 s, stats 5 to 10 min, geo 10 to 30 min, mesh 30 to 60 min). The thresholds are the same for every surface, so one rule explains every chip.

| State | Age | Look |
|---|---|---|
| fresh | under 1.5x cadence | `--text-3`, ok dot |
| aging | 1.5x to 3x | `--text-2`, no dot |
| stale | over 3x | `--status-warn`, the word "stale" |
| dead | over 10x | `--status-crit` |

Age reads "now", "4 s", "41 s", "12 min", "3 h". One 1 Hz timer drives every chip (never per-chip timers), chips slow to a 10 s cadence past one minute, and seconds swap instantly without rolling.

**Connection indicator** (status bar, left).

| State | Look | Copy |
|---|---|---|
| Live | green dot with a slow 2.4 s ping ring | "Live, 41 ms" (round-trip EMA from ping and pong) |
| Syncing | blue spinner | "Catching up, 12 blocks" |
| Reconnecting | amber dot blinking at 1 Hz, 50% duty; a 2 px amber line under the top bar | "Reconnecting in 3 s" with a live countdown |
| Offline | red dot with a wifi-off icon; banner in the top bar; all freshness chips go stale; live effects stop; the moon keeps orbiting but its ring stops | "Offline, showing data from 14:02:11" |
| Upstream stale | amber dot | "Server is up, upstream data is 4 min old" (from `stale: true`) |

Returning to Live requires 2 s of stability (no flapping). No toast for reconnecting; a feed row only after an outage over 10 s.

**Counters.** Top-level counters (nodes, hosts, apps, tier counts, height, price) coalesce to at most one visual update per second, tint by direction, and use the odometer (6.4 D).

**Announcements.** The feed is `aria-live="polite"` and announces at most one summary per 5 s ("3 new events") rather than every row. Block landings announce once ("Block 2,997,012 produced by a Stratus node, paid to three nodes"). Assistive users can switch the live region off in Settings.

## 7. Globe art-direction contract

This section is the contract between design and the renderer (labs/globe, later `web/src/globe`). The renderer owns implementation, performance and shader craft; design owns what it looks like, how it moves, which colours it reads and what it must expose. Numbers are starting points: the lab may propose different values with a screenshot, and approved changes land back here and in `tokens.css`. The design mock's Canvas2D renderer (`mock/assets/globe.js` and `moon.js`) is the visual reference for every number below that it implements.

Colours are read from the `--globe-*` tokens (runtime: `getComputedStyle(document.documentElement)`; offline: `docs/design/tokens.json` keys `globe`, `tier` and `hot`). The renderer never hard-codes a colour. The palette is the Flux brand: a planet of Blue Wave dots on Flux Black, white as the attention colour, tier colours only where a payout arrives, and the moon (7.10) in brand colours only.

### 7.1 Role and principles

1. **The globe is the desktop.** One instance, mounted once, alive for the whole session (boot to ambient). Windows float over it. It never unmounts on route changes.
2. **Calm at rest.** With no events, Z0 is almost still: slow drift, no flicker, no per-node shimmer. Life comes from events (something happened), not from noise. The one exception is the moon's slow orbit.
3. **Hierarchy of light.** Background sphere dim, land dots dimmer than nodes, nodes dimmer than events, events dimmer than selection. Selection is the brightest thing in the frame, always.
4. **Real data only.** Every mark is a real node, edge or event. Nothing decorative moves like data.
5. **Legible before pretty, pretty before clever.** Colour-blind safe (size and rings differ by tier), readable on a dim laptop panel, and no effect may hide a node under it.
6. **Tokens, not literals.** Every colour and most alphas come from `--globe-*`.
7. **The moon is the chain, and it is part of the picture.** It is drawn by the renderer, always in front, always on screen, only in brand colours, and it is the one object that is also a control (7.10).

### 7.2 Camera and coordinates

- Lat and lon in degrees, +lon east, north up by default. Equirectangular land mask and density textures. The sphere is radius R = 1.
- The camera orbits the centre; the pose is `{lat, lon, size, tilt, roll}` where **size** is the apparent globe diameter as a percentage of viewport height (FOV independent). Default overview 78% (the shell's bare pose is 100% with roll -14 degrees), hero (boot, ambient) 92 to 96%, minimum 52%, maximum 1,400%.
- Controls: drag to orbit with inertia (damping 0.92), wheel and pinch zoom on a log scale, double click zooms to the point, arrow keys orbit, `+`/`-` zoom, `Home` resets. Idle drift 1.2 degrees per second, stopped by any input, resumed after 20 s of idleness (never when a window is focused or Motion is reduced).
- **Viewport inset** `setInset({left, right, top, bottom}, ms)`: the free area not covered by docked UI. The globe centres in the free area and eases 300 ms `--ease-in-out` when docks change; the moon's ring re-fits to the same area (7.10.4) and the aim strip re-centres. Flying to a node centres it at the free area's horizontal centre.
- Flights: duration `900 + 1700 * min(1, angle / 120deg)` ms, `--ease-in-out`, with an altitude arc (size dips by up to 18% mid-flight on long hops). Flights are cancellable.
- **Zoom bands** by `size`: Z0 global, under 110%; Z1 continental, 110 to 260%; Z2 regional, 260 to 700%; Z3 city, over 700%. Every LOD rule below keys off these.

### 7.5 Co-location: stacks and fans

The node list has two co-location levels. **Sites** are the geolocated points shared by many hosts (datacenter cities; `loc` in `nodes.bin`). **Hosts** are single IPs carrying up to 8 UPnP nodes (ports 16127 to 16197, 64% of all nodes).

| Zoom | Site rendering | Host and node rendering |
|---|---|---|
| Z0 | density glow (7.6) plus **stack towers** for the top 60 sites | individual points at 55% alpha, 1 px core |
| Z1 | towers for sites with 8 or more nodes, count labels for the top 25 | individual points |
| Z2 | towers shrink to short stubs, site pin with city label | individual points; sites with more than 1 node draw a small cluster ring |
| Z3 | site pin only | **fan**: nodes spread in screen space around the site pin |

**Tower.** Vertical line from the surface along the normal, height `min(0.16R, 0.018R x (1 + ln n))`, so 2 nodes is a 0.03R stub and 40 nodes about 0.11R. Width 1.6 px at Z0 and Z1, 3 px at Z2. Segmented bottom to top by tier (Stratus at the base, then Nimbus, then Cumulus) with segment lengths proportional to count, each segment in its tier colour at 85% with a 1 px gap. A bright cap (core-sized, white-hot centre) tops the tower. Towers are additive and depth tested against the sphere. Hover lifts a tower 8% and shows its card; click zooms to its bounds.

**Fan (Z3).** Nodes animate from the site centre into a Vogel spiral (golden angle) in screen space, hosts first: hosts sit on rings 28 px apart, a host's nodes are 7 px apart around the host point, each node has a 0.5 px leader at 25% alpha to its host centre, so the UPnP "port ladder" shows up geometrically. Fan transitions run 360 ms `--ease-out-expo` with 8 ms stagger; collapse is the reverse. The fan is billboarded and keeps a 0.5 px leader line to the true site pin.

### 7.8 Events: arcs, beams, pulses

All events use one geodesic primitive: a great-circle arc sampled in 48 to 96 segments, lifted by `alt(d) = a0 + a1 * (d / 180deg)` (R units), drawn as a screen-space ribbon (width in px), additive, depth tested against the sphere so far-side parts are hidden. Arc parameters: `head` (0..1 along the path), `trail` (fraction), `width`, colour at tail and head. The two legs of the moon relay (7.10.5) are the exception: they are streaks along a world-space route between the planet and the moon, because the moon is not on the sphere.

| Event | Look | Timing |

| **Block landing** | the relay of 6.4 I and 7.10.5: producer flare and pillar (0.34R), shockwave ring to 62 degrees that lights nodes as it passes (+35% for 300 ms), uplink to the moon, the moon receives, the bar's chip and three downlinks in coinbase order (Cumulus, Nimbus, Stratus), arrival pulses and amount chips | 0 to 2780 ms (chips to 4540) |
| **Shockwave** | ring of angular radius `reach x easeOutCubic(u)` around the producer, a luminous swell rather than a hairline: a soft glow about 40 px wide, a 16 px shoulder, a wash that trails behind the front and dies away, and a crisp 5 px band with a hot core (`--globe-beam-head`) that only the young wave has, so it starts as a crisp front and ends as a soft glow; all in `--globe-shock`, alpha `(1 - u)^1.7`; `reach` `--globe-shock-reach` (62 degrees), `--dur-shock` (1700 ms) (emission: a second ring at +400 ms, `--globe-shock-reach-emission` 88 degrees, `--dur-shock-emission` 2100 ms) | 1700 ms |

**Mesh (gossip) layer.** `Mesh` has three modes.
- `Off`.
- `Selection` (default when a node is selected): the node's peers radiate as arcs. Outbound: solid gradient with a small travelling packet (2 px dot, 0.25 cycles per second, random phase). Inbound: dotted (2 px dash, 4 px gap). Arcs `a0` 0.05R, `a1` 0.18R, width 1.2 px, `--globe-mesh` at `--globe-mesh-alpha` (.22), brighter toward the peer. At most 60 arcs; peers sorted by latency, the rest count into a "+N more" chip.
- `Flow` (network-wide): every edge of `mesh.bin` as a luminous web, edge-bundled through regional control points, `--globe-mesh` at `--globe-mesh-flow-alpha` (.05) additive, plus about 300 travelling packets at a time. Off by default at Z2 and above (too dense). Cross-continent edges (`flags` bit1) are 1.6x brighter. This is the ambient hero layer.

### 7.9 Overlays and modes

- **App constellation**: instance nodes get white-hot cores with tier-coloured rims and 4-point glints (6 px, slow twinkle); edges are the minimum spanning tree over great-circle distance, 1 px `--globe-app` (white) at .6, lying at altitude 0.01R with 6 px gaps at nodes; a label at the centroid ("BitcoinWhitepaper, 3 instances, 3 countries"); all other nodes dim. Selecting draws the tree progressively (BFS order) over 900 ms.
- **Operator view**: owned nodes get the Mine ring, everything else dims; MST lines in `--globe-mine` at .45; a payment-ETA ring (arc progress) fills around a node over its final 120 blocks before payment; payments play the relay.
- **Archive grade (time machine)**: desaturate 35% toward `--globe-archive-tint` (`--globe-archive-amount`), scanlines at 2 px period alpha .035, vignette +.15. Live events are suppressed; replayed events draw at 70% intensity. The moon takes the grade, hides its ring and plays no relay. A `t minus` chip is drawn by the UI.
- **Layers** (all toggles, persisted): Nodes, Density, Stacks, Mesh (off, selection, flow), Arcs (events), Apps, ArcaneOS, Day and night, Graticule, Borders, Labels, Hubs, Aim, Weather, **Moon** (on by default; off hides the moon and its hit target, and is for screenshots and kiosks, not a user-facing default).
- **Ambient**: see 7.11.

### 7.10 The Moon

The Flux symbol orbits the planet as the chain itself. Every 30 seconds a producer node hands it a block, and it pays three nodes. It is drawn by the globe renderer (so the boot can assemble it in the same canvas, 7.10.9) and is reachable as a control through a DOM proxy (7.10.6). Brand rule zero: only brand colours are on or touching it (7.10.11).

**7.10.1 The piece map.** The symbol is four official paths; each stands for one output of the block's coinbase.

| Output | Piece | Official path | Stands for | Paid per block | Face colour | Its beam | Flashes when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Slanted bar | Path_73 | dev-fund output | 0.5 FLUX plus the block's fees | `--globe-moon-spark` (`#FFFFFF`) | no node: a drift pulse and a chip "+0.50 dev fund" | first, before any beam (830 ms) |
| 1 | Small hexagon | Path_76 | Cumulus output | 1 FLUX | `--globe-moon-cumulus` (`#7E7C7C`) | `--globe-cumulus` | the Cumulus beam leaves (960 ms) |

The geometry is the official one, unredrawn: mark space 279.714 x 322.975 (`FluxBrand.PIECES` in `mock/assets/brand.js`, each path carrying its own translate), drawn with its true proportions. The slanted bar is the piece at the bottom. The cap, the widest piece, pays the most and the small hexagon the least, so the four pieces read as the coinbase in miniature, and they fire in its own order, output 0 to 3, so the relay builds from the smallest piece to the cap.

**7.10.2 Size and proportion.** The moon is a world object with a world size, so it obeys perspective like the planet does. Its symbol is `0.22 R` tall in the shell (`0.28 R` as the viewport turns portrait: the phone and a tablet held upright) and `0.36 R` in the sky orbit (7.10.12). It grows as the camera comes in, shrinks as it goes out, and is larger on the near half of its lap than on the far half: on a 1600 x 900 viewport at the default zoom it is 53 to 91 px tall (mean 73), and 3.7 lists the other layouts. It is never a constant screen size. Two soft limits keep it a control and not a landmark: a floor of 40 px (legibility and a touch target; the phone's `moonScale` 0.86 does not lower it) and an eased ceiling of `clamp(0.26 x min(viewport w, h), 110, 240)` px, so a camera pushed against it never lets it swallow the frame (a cinematic shot lifts the ceiling and it returns eased, never as a jump). Seen from far away (the wide landing shot) it grows by up to 45% so it stays a presence in the frame. The width is 0.866 of the height. The hexagonal ring around it has a circumradius of 0.70 of the height. The tokens `--globe-moon-size`, `--globe-moon-size-min` and `--globe-moon-size-max` belong to the Canvas2D reference, which draws a constant screen size; the WebGL renderer does not read them.

**7.10.3 Rendering recipe (the shell look; the Canvas2D reference, ported to WebGL with the changes noted).** One light source at the top left, as everywhere in Atlas.

1. **Glow**: a pre-rendered radial sprite (160 px, `--globe-moon-glow` stops 0.95, 0.5, 0.16, 0.04, 0) drawn additively at 3.3 x the moon's height with alpha `--globe-moon-glow-alpha` (0.55) x (0.70 to 1.00 breathing, a 4.8 s period) + 0.4 x hover + 0.5 x flash. On a flash a second, white halo sprite at 2.0 x height and 0.55 x flash alpha is added.
2. **The chain and the wake (the orbit itself is never drawn: no dotted hairline, no ring, no guide line).** Two things on the real orbit, in world space, carry the sense of motion. **The wake** is a comet's tail: it starts at the moon's trailing edge (nothing of it crosses the mark), is brightest and widest right there and thins to nothing about 26 degrees of orbit back (its width follows the moon's size on screen, in device pixels); a white-hot core inside a Flux blue body inside a soft glow, a slow shimmer of light flowing backward along it, and a pulse of light that runs down its length when a block is sealed. **The chain** is one bead for every block the moon has sealed, born at the moon's angle at the instant of the seal: a tiny glowing hexagon (the symbol's own shape, with a smaller hexagon inside it, a block within the block), flashing white, kicked out from behind the moon and falling back along the orbit with a slight tumble, cooling from white to Flux light blue to Flux blue and fading like an ember over 0.4 of a lap (96 s), so a handful are alive at once (one per 30 s block). Beads are never evenly spaced (each block has its own size, scatter and fall), never joined by a line and never a ring. A bead exists because a block was sealed: no tier colour, no text, never decoration. Both hold their place when the camera moves and pass in front of and behind the planet with the moon, fading at its limb. The blocks of the last 96 s are seeded at load. The chain dims to 20% while an app constellation or an operator fan is on screen, so their labels keep the room. Neither is drawn in the lite tier or under reduced motion.
3. **Ring** (the block clock): a pointy-top hexagon, circumradius 0.70 of the height, a hairline track in `--globe-moon-ring` (`#86A1DA`) at 16% and 1.1 px; over it the progress stroke (the block clock, 0 to 1 clockwise from the top vertex) in two additive passes, a 5 px glow at 22% (+40% x flash) and a 1.7 px core at 85% (+15% x flash), and a 2.3 px white dot at its head.
4. **Extrusion**: the four paths repeated N times, N = `round(clamp(4 + height / 14, 5, 11))`, each copy shifted by `(0.62, 0.78) x depth x f` for f from 1/N to 1 (down and to the right, away from the light), depth = `--globe-moon-depth` (0.14) x symbol height, filled with a ramp from `--globe-moon-edge-hi` through `--globe-moon-edge` (`#2B61D1`, the side walls) darkened toward black by up to 62% at the back.
5. **Faces**: each piece filled with its tonal face colour (table above), then clipped and overlaid with a top-left highlight (linear gradient: white 30% at the top left, clear by 42%, deep blue `#000828` at 34% at the bottom right), a **specular sweep** (a diagonal white band at 50%, about 160 units wide, crossing the faces every 12 s), and a 1.15 px bevel stroke in `--globe-moon-edge-hi` at 60%.
6. **Sway**: the whole symbol turns about its vertical axis by up to 11 degrees each way (a 27 s period), as a horizontal scale by the cosine and a shear of 0.25 x the sine, which is what shows the depth. Zero in reduced motion.
7. **Flash state**: a piece flash or the receive flash mixes a face toward `#FFFFFF` (by `0.9 x piece flash` or `0.8 x receive flash`) for the flash's duration, with the glow and ring brightened as above. A **firing piece also blooms**: a white halo about 340 mark units wide behind it at 70% x its flash, and its bevel stroke thickens by up to 80% and turns white. The bloom is white on Blue Wave, never a tier colour; the lite tier skips it.

**7.10.4 Orbit and placement.** The moon is a **world object on a real orbit**, not a companion locked to the camera. It circles the planet's centre in the planet's own frame, prograde (east, the way the planet turns), and its position is a function of the UTC clock and nothing else (`theta = 2 pi x (t mod period) / period`), so every viewer sees it in the same place at the same moment and the camera never moves it along its path. Panning, tilting and zooming only change the view of an orbit that stays where it is: the moon holds its place on its path when the planet is dragged round, it parallaxes against the planet as any near object does, it follows perspective in size (7.10.2), it can leave the screen when the camera is pushed in or panned away, and once a lap it passes behind the planet.

- **Two shapes of one circle.** The **shell ring** (explore mode, desktop and phone) is a circle of 1.5 R (half a radius above the surface) in a plane that the default camera sees as a gently rolled ellipse (rolled 11 degrees and opened 13 degrees on a landscape screen, opened 18 degrees on a portrait one). It is fitted so that the whole lap sits in the free area at the default zoom: the viewport minus the insets (the top bar, the status bar and rail, the dock and the inspector; on the phone the aim chips row and the sheet's top edge), less the moon's own clearance (0.74 of its height). The design's landscape ring is kept wherever it fits at full size; otherwise the fit turns the plane as little as it must (up to upright: a phone gets a tall ring, and a docked inspector on a small screen stands it up) and only then shrinks the radius (never under 1.06 R). The fit uses the live insets: when `setInset` changes (a window docks, the inspector opens, the viewport resizes) the ring glides to its new fit on a critically damped spring and never jumps. The **sky orbit** (ambient and the cinematic shots) is a wider, steeper circle (2.05 R, inclined 27 degrees) that the camera frames (7.10.12). The blend between the two (900 ms, `--ease-in-out`) interpolates radius, inclination, node and size, and every blend of two circles is itself a circle, so the moon sweeps between them without a pop. The lap is `--globe-moon-orbit-s` (240 s) in every mode, ambient included: one UTC clock and one period, so the moon never jumps when the mode changes.
- **The planet hides it, softly.** The planet's disc is an analytic occluder (no depth test, so nothing fights the atmosphere): the moon and its wake, beads and beams fade over a band outside the limb (`max(0.035 R, 2 px)`) and are hidden while behind it, which at the default view is about a third of every lap. The relay does not wait for the moon: it plays in any pose (7.10.5), and a beam to a moon on the far side climbs over the limb and disappears behind the disc.
- **Past the default view** the moon is simply a real object: it may pass behind a window (windows are in front of the canvas) or leave the screen. The DOM proxy, the tooltip and the About tether follow its projected position every frame; the proxy grows with the moon's perspective size (never under 44 px), dims while the planet hides it and stays reachable by keyboard, as does `M` (7.10.6). The pointer takes the moon only while its visibility is above 0.6.
- Depth cue: true perspective in size only (7.10.2); no change in brightness.
- Phone: the ring is fitted to the sector between the aim chips row and the sheet's top edge; when the sheet is at tall or full the moon parks as a 24 px symbol in the header's Beat mini (3.6).
- Reduced motion: no lap. The moon is parked at a seat on its orbit chosen (from 360 samples of the lap) to be in view and clear of the planet, and if the planet is turned so that the seat would be hidden it re-seats with a cross-fade. No sway, no wake, no chain, no sweep, no recoil.

A second block within 3 s plays uplink and downlinks only (1.2 s).

**7.10.7 Ambient.** The moon is the only chrome that stays in ambient mode. It moves to the sky orbit (7.10.12): bigger (0.36 R, about 1.6 times the shell's world size) and wider (2.05 R, inclined 27 degrees), on the same 240 s lap and the same UTC clock as everywhere, so it is where every other viewer sees it; the lap is not halved, because a second period would make the moon jump when the mode changes. The scene director frames it with the cinematic moon shots (portrait, earthrise, eclipse, follow) and the Landing shots for the relay, which continues exactly as in live mode; the ring keeps its clock, and the hit target and tooltip are off. The ambient sentence ("Block 2,996,930 came from Reston. Paid to Helsinki, Raleigh and Taganrog.") is set in Lora. The Canvas2D reference keeps an expanded camera-locked companion in ambient (1.35 x, 120 s lap); the WebGL renderer uses the sky orbit there (7.10.12).

**7.10.8 Interaction with the free area and windows.** The shell ring re-fits whenever `setInset` changes (the insets ease over 300 ms and the ring glides after them), so opening the inspector slides the lap out of its way and closing it releases it: at the default zoom a docked window never covers the moon. Once the camera is moved the moon is a world object and may pass behind a window or off the screen (7.10.4). The About window's tether starts just outside the moon's hexagon ring at its upper right (0.54 of the height up and right of its centre, pulled in 13 px along the diagonal) and follows the moon's projected position each frame.

**7.10.9 The boot: assemble, lift off, become the moon.** The renderer draws the boot symbol with the same four paths and the same code as the moon, so there is no hand-off. `setMoonBoot(o)` controls it: `o.pieces` is the arrival progress of each piece, in the order bar, cap, big hexagon, small hexagon (0 far away and invisible, 1 home); a piece en route comes from a fixed direction (the bar from below, the cap from above, the big hexagon from the right, the small hexagon from the left), moves with easeOutBack and a few degrees of rotation, and its outline is drawn dashed in `--globe-moon-edge-hi` until it lands; `o.cx, o.cy, o.size` place the assembling symbol (250 px, 150 on phone); `o.white` is the "whole" flash (0 to 1); `o.lift` 0 to 1 runs the lift-off: position eases `--ease-in-out` from (cx, cy) to the moon's live orbit position along an arc that rises 12% of the viewport height, size eases from `o.size` to the moon's size, and the look changes from **flat white** (all faces `#FFFFFF`, no depth, no glow, no ring) to the full moon (tonal faces, extrusion, glow, ring). While `o.lift` is below 1 the moon's own orbit phase keeps advancing so the lift lands on the orbit, not on a fixed point. `setMoonBoot(null)` ends the boot; the moon is then drawn by `drawMoon` with no visible change. The reveal wave is `setReveal(nodeId, thetaRad)`: only land and nodes within `theta` of the origin are drawn, and a thin ring marks the front; `setReveal(null)` ends it. The sequence, timing and log are in 6.4 J and 9.1.

**7.10.10 Lite tier and cost.** In the lite tier (`data-perf="lite"`, `--globe-moon-depth` 0) the moon is drawn flat: no extrusion, glow, gradient overlays, sweep, bevel, chain or wake, in the tonal faces (one white fill is an acceptable fallback); ring and relay stay. Budget: the moon costs at most 0.5 ms per frame in Canvas2D on the reference machine (one sprite, up to 44 path fills for the extrusion, 4 face fills and clips, 2 ring strokes) and one draw call for the extrusion plus one for the faces in WebGL.

**7.10.12 The shell ring and the sky orbit (reconciling the Globe Lab's 3D moon).** The lab renders the moon as a true world-space object (lit by the planet's sun, eclipsed by the planet, with a chain of beads and cinematic moon shots: portrait, earthrise, eclipse, follow). The interactive shell needs the same object to stay a control and the relay's actor. The renderer therefore draws one world object in two shapes, and the UI never notices (the older names for the two were companion mode and orbit mode; `mode: 'companion'` and `mode: 'orbit'` still select them):

| | **Shell ring** (the interactive shell, desktop and phone) | **Sky orbit** (ambient, the lab stage, the gallery, cinematic moon shots) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | a control and the relay's actor: legible, reachable and out of the way at the default view | a celestial body with real depth: parallax, phases, eclipses, the planet's shadow |

| Lap | `period` 240 s on the UTC clock | the same |
| Size | a world size, 0.22 R (0.28 R portrait): perspective, never a constant screen size (7.10.2) | 0.36 R: perspective, framed by the camera |

Rules. (1) The shell runs the shell ring; `setMode('ambient')` switches to the sky orbit (the lab's stage and the gallery start in it). `setMoon({mode: 'companion' | 'orbit'})` (or `placement`: `auto`, `compact`, `sky`) overrides both. (2) The switch is a **blend**, not a cut: over `--dur-ambient-out` (900 ms, `--ease-in-out`) the orbit's radius, inclination, node and size interpolate (every blend of two circles is a circle) and the look eases, so the moon sweeps from one shape to the other and never pops; the planet may hide it in passing, softly. (3) Both shapes are the same mesh and material: the symbol is a camera-facing billboard, upright on the screen at any pose, placed at its orbit point in the world and drawn in an overlay pass after a depth clear, with the planet's occlusion computed in the shader. (4) Everything in the brand rules (7.10.11), the relay (6.4 I, in coinbase order) and the boot hand-off (7.10.9) is identical in both shapes. (5) The cinematic moon shots are ambient-only; leaving ambient releases the camera (`releaseCamera`) while the blend runs. (6) Whether the glass treatment, the phases and the eclipse pass the brand owner's sign-off is open question 1 in 12.5; the flat white symbol remains the fallback in both shapes.

| Constellation | 10 to 14 s | fit to bounds, slow push-in | a random top-20 app, tree drawing, label |
| Counters | 6 s | orbit continues | the UI overlay counts up nodes, apps, height |

Pacing: transitions 2.4 s `--ease-in-out`, never two high-energy scenes in a row, and event density steers (a burst of events shortens scenes and favours Landing; quiet favours Orbit and Web). Scene boundaries are cued to block events where possible so the camera cuts on the shockwave. Bloom and star layers may use extra budget here. Camera drift and overlay drift (6 px per 60 s) also prevent burn-in on TVs; the moon's lap (240 s) is part of that.

### 7.16 Design acceptance checklist (how the lab output is judged)

1. **Calm**: at Z0 for 10 s with no events, nothing flickers and nothing moves except slow drift and the moon's orbit.
2. **Hierarchy**: selection is the brightest element, then event heads, then nodes, then land, then graticule.
3. **Tier reads** at a glance in a dense region, and in a greyscale screenshot (size and rings carry it).
4. **Stacks**: a hub such as a Hetzner city reads as a tower whose colours match the tier mix; zooming shows the same count split into hosts.
5. **Landing**: the producer flare, shockwave lighting the nodes it passes, the uplink, the moon's receive and the three downlinks read as one gesture in under 2.8 s, in the coinbase's order, and a second landing inside 3 s degrades gracefully.
6. **Mesh**: the selection arcs are legible with 40 peers; `Flow` looks like a luminous web, not a hairball, and never hides nodes.
7. **Depth**: arcs and towers are occluded correctly by the sphere; nothing floats through it. The moon and its relay curves are the only things drawn over the sphere without occlusion, by design.
8. **Colour**: no region clips to flat white; the dim state is actually dim (alpha .14).
9. **Budget**: `stats()` shows 60 fps on the reference machine in `balanced`, and `lite` holds 60 on a weak iGPU.
10. **Reduced motion**: no drift, no travelling packets, no orbit, landing reduced to a static flare and static relay lines (6.6).
11. **The moon**: on screen in every pose and zoom (check Z0 and a Z3 city view); never overlapped by a window; only brand colours on it in a colour-picked screenshot; the beams leave it in tier colours; hover slows it; the DOM proxy focus ring is visible.
12. **Land**: the planet reads as a dot matrix at every zoom, with antialiased coasts, two LODs with no pop at R 640, and a clean fade by R 3200.
13. **Boot**: the assembled symbol is pixel-identical to the moon's first frame (no pop), and the lift lands on the orbit.

## 9. Key interaction flows

### 9.2 Search, fly to, inspect

The visitor presses Control and K (or clicks the omnibox) and types `65.109`.

1. The palette opens in `--dur-base` (220 ms): a 660 px panel, the field focused, "Recent" and "Try" groups under an empty query.
2. Results appear locally within a frame and from the server within about 150 ms: Nodes (`65.109.26.93:16147` Stratus, Helsinki, next in line, with a green "Confirmed" chip; three more on the same range), "Hosts and providers" (Host `65.109.26.93`, 7 nodes, 1 operator; Hetzner Online GmbH, AS24940, 1,614 nodes, 24% of the network), "Go to" (Fly to Helsinki, 491 nodes at this site; Open operator `t1cz5P...Dg9`; About Flux).

4. The camera flies (900 to 2,600 ms by distance, an altitude arc), the marker gains a beacon (a white pillar and two ring pulses), and when the target is about 65% of the way there the node inspector opens out of the marker through the aperture (6.4 A) with its tether drawing in 180 to 700 ms.

1. The visitor presses T. The timeline strip grows from 22 to 76 px (360 ms), the globe takes the archive grade (600 ms), the Beat chip becomes "t minus", and the moon hides its ring (it is the present's clock).

### 9.10 Losing and regaining the stream

1. The socket drops. The status bar's connection chip goes amber, "Reconnecting, retry in 4 s" with a live countdown, and a 2 px amber line runs under the top bar. No toast: nothing is interrupted.
2. Data stops updating and every freshness chip begins to age; values keep their last number and dim to `--text-3` with "as of 14:02:11". The moon keeps orbiting but its ring stops and its glow drops to 0.25, and the Beat chip says "block late" only if the chain is also late.
3. After 10 s without a stream a feed row says "Stream lost for 10 s".
4. The socket reopens. The chip returns to Live only after 2 s of stability, the client asks for the blocks it missed ("Catching up, 12 blocks"), the rail fills in without the relay (only the newest block plays it), and "Reconnected" may unlock (9.9).
5. If the Atlas server is up but its Flux source is behind, the chip reads "Upstream stale, Flux API 4 min behind" and a state panel appears where live data matters; nothing is presented as fresher than it is.

## 10. Accessibility

Atlas targets WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere and AAA for body text on the main slab. The interface is dark-only (`color-scheme: dark`), so contrast is designed once and verified on the five surfaces. The two things that need extra care are a canvas globe that draws most of the picture and a moon that is both an image and a control.

### 10.2 Focus, targets and focus order

- **Focus ring**: `--focus-ring` everywhere (a 2 px `--ink-0` gap and a 2 px `--accent-400` ring, 7.7:1 against the slab); on chamfered shapes a 2 px outline with a 3 px offset. Focus is never removed and never colour alone (the ring is a shape). The moon's proxy adds a Blue Wave glow.
- **Targets**: 32 px minimum on pointer devices (`--hit-min`), 44 px on touch (`--touch-min`). Smaller visuals get a larger hit area (a 26 px small button sits in a 32 px row; a 7 px dot is never a target). The moon's target is at least 44 px (78 px across on a 1600 by 900 desktop) and its pick circle wins over any node beneath it (7.10.6).
- **Order**: skip link ("Skip to the globe"), the top bar left to right (lockup, menus, omnibox, Beat chip, Live chip, icons, operator chip), the dock top to bottom, the aim strip, the Pulse card, the rail (newest first), the status bar, the moon's proxy button, then any windows in stacking order (the docked one last). Opening a window moves focus to its title; closing it returns focus to the control that opened it (the dock item, the row, the moon's proxy). Esc closes the topmost layer, and focus never lands on the canvas unexpectedly.
- **Traps**: only the palette and modal dialogs trap focus; windows are non-modal and reachable in the tab order.
- **Pointer alternatives**: every drag has a keyboard or button equivalent (windows snap with `Alt` and the arrow keys or the dock button; the timeline handle takes arrow keys; the moon opens with M).
- **Text resize**: sizes are in rem; the layout works at 200% zoom and 320 CSS px wide; windows go to phone sheets under 720 px.

### 10.3 Screen readers: the canvas globe, the moon, the live regions

**The globe.** The canvas is a picture of data that is always available as text. It has `role="img"` and a label that summarises the state ("Live globe of all Flux nodes: 6,724 nodes in 54 countries; chain tip 2,996,929; drag to rotate, scroll to zoom"), updated at most once a minute. Nothing exists only on the canvas: every node, app, block and payee is reachable through the omnibox, the tables and the inspector, and every inspector has a "Fly to" button. Keyboard orbit and zoom work with focus on the canvas (arrows, `+` and `-`, `Home`). Selection state is announced ("Selected 65.109.26.93:16147, Stratus, Helsinki") when it changes.

**The moon.** The canvas draws it; a transparent `<button>` over it (the DOM proxy, 7.10.6) makes it a control: `aria-label="About Flux, live network totals"`, `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, reachable by Tab and M, activated by Enter or Space. Its tooltip is an `aria-describedby` target while it is visible and does not announce the ticking countdown; the tooltip is **dismissible** (Esc), **hoverable** (it stays while the pointer is on it) and **persistent** until focus or pointer leaves (WCAG 1.4.13). The moon's ring is decorative for assistive technology because the Beat chip carries the same clock in words.

**The relay in words.** The block landing is announced **once**, in a polite status region, when its last beam arrives: "Block 2,996,930 produced by a Cumulus node in Copenhagen. Paid to Helsinki 9.00, Raleigh 3.50 and Taganrog 1.00." For a watched or owned node the toast is a separate `role="status"`: "Payment received, plus 9.00 FLUX". No announcement is made per beam, per chip or per tick.

| Surface | Semantics |
|---|---|
| Pulse feed | `aria-live="polite"`, at most one summary per 5 s ("3 new events"), rows are list items with the time as `<time>`; hover-freeze also stops announcements; Settings can turn the region off |
| Aim strip | `aria-live="polite"`, announces only when the three payees change ("Next payout in 18 s: Helsinki 9.0, Raleigh 3.5, Taganrog 1.0"), never the countdown |
| Status bar chips | `role="status"`; freshness chips are updated silently; a change of connection state is announced once |
| Toasts | `role="status"` (payments, achievements) or `role="alert"` (errors), dismissible, pause on hover and focus |
| Windows | floating: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal="false"`, `aria-labelledby` the title; docked inspector: `role="complementary"` with a label; tabs `tablist`, `tab`, `tabpanel`; the tether is `aria-hidden` |

### 10.5 Motion, contrast and other preferences

- **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` or Settings): the moon is parked with no orbit, sway or chain; the relay is static lines that fade in and out; the boot cross-fades; nothing loops; transitions are opacity only; the full table is in 6.6. The user can force Full.
- **Contrast**: 10.1. **Forced colours** (Windows High Contrast): windows and controls keep 1 px system-colour borders (`forced-color-adjust: none` only on the canvas), focus uses the system focus colour, status chips keep their icons and words.
- **Colour scheme**: dark only by design; there is no light theme, and the brand's light page colour (`--flux-paper`) appears only on the About footer card if one is added.
- **Performance tier** (Settings): Auto, High, Balanced, Lite; Lite removes blur, grain and the moon's depth, and keeps every function.
- **Cognitive load**: one loud motion per 30 seconds; no auto-advancing carousels; bursts collapse; the Pulse can be turned off; all copy is sentence case and plain.
- **Zoom and text size**: 200% zoom and OS text scaling supported; fixed-height rows use `min-height` so larger text wraps rather than clips.

### 10.6 Colour-blind and greyscale notes

- **Tier** is encoded four ways: colour, the capsule glyph (one, two or three capsules), the ring count around a node (none, one, two) and the word. The validator results for the tier trio are in 5.2: adjacent CVD dE 10.5, normal-vision dE 18.1. A greyscale screenshot still separates the tiers by size, rings and lightness (Stratus brightest, Nimbus darkest).
- **The relay** colours its beams by tier, but the order (bar, small hexagon, big hexagon, cap), the piece that flashes, and the amount chips ("+1.00", "+3.50", "+9.00") carry the same information without colour.
- **Status** is an icon plus a word; "ok" and "crit" also differ in shape (circle-check against octagon-x).
- **Charts**: six categorical slots in a fixed order (the order is the CVD-safety mechanism), direct labels, and end-of-line markers; the diverging palette is paired with arrows and signs; sequential ramps are lightness-monotone.
- **The moon** uses lightness only (white, light grey, grey faces on Blue Wave), so it is identical under every colour-vision deficiency.

### What to build first

The order that gets the signature on screen soonest and lets each step reuse the last: the moon (static, then orbiting), then the relay (rank 1), the boot (2) which reuses the moon's paths and lighting, the aperture and tether (4) which About Flux needs, pre-aim (5), ambient (6), the reward cut (3) in time for block 3,071,200, then the heavy data features (7, 8, 9). Items 10 to 13 ride along with the components that contain them.

### If time runs short

Cut from the bottom of the list, and within the globe cut in this order: producer heat, mesh version wave, flapping links, night lights, weather layer. Never cut the relay or the boot: they are the reason to open the product, and the moon's styling has a built-in fallback (the flat white symbol, 7.10.10) if the lit rendering is not approved by the brand owner.

### The 90-second demo

1. **0 to 12 s**, boot: the symbol builds itself from the log and lifts into orbit.
2. **12 to 30 s**, wait for the block: point at the Beat ring filling on the moon and in the top bar; the first relay lands ("producer, moon, three payees, dev fund").
3. **30 to 45 s**, click the moon: About Flux opens from it; the anatomy row explains which piece pays what.
4. **45 to 65 s**, Control and K, `65.109`: the flight, the aperture, the inspector, "7 nodes, one host".
5. **65 to 80 s**, Q: the payment queue wheel, "next in 14.7 hours".
6. **80 to 90 s**, Shift and A: the interface dissolves, the moon grows, the sentence names the last payees.

### 12.5 Open questions

1. **The moon's styling needs the brand owner's sign-off.** The extrusion, bevel, lighting, glow, the hexagon ring, the chain of beads, the wake and the world orbit are an interpretation of the official symbol, which the Brand Book shows only flat; the Globe Lab's glass material, phases and eclipses are a second interpretation (7.10.12). The fallback, used in the lite tier and acceptable everywhere, is the flat white symbol. The same question covers whether the symbol may move at all.
2. **Naming of the 0.5 FLUX output.** The mock says "Dev fund" and "development fund". Confirm the ecosystem's preferred term. Also confirm where transaction fees go: `explorer-api.md` observed the dev output at 0.5000003 with a 3e-7 fee and reads the coinbase rule as "0.5 plus all fees"; `flux-api.md` says fee handling was not traced. Until confirmed the UI says "0.5 FLUX dev fund" and "plus fees" appears only in About Flux's caption and this document; a transaction's fee is labelled "Network fee" and nothing says the producer is paid a fee (it gets no extra reward).
3. **Search and transaction semantics** follow `explorer-api.md` (resolution order, the coinbase's four outputs, `minedBy` is the Stratus payee and never "miner"); what remains open is how to show shielded transactions and how to attribute `t3` multisig collateral to operators.
4. **Tablet portrait** (3.5) is described but not mocked; tablet landscape 1180 is mocked.
5. **The reference globe is Canvas2D**, not the production WebGL renderer. Its numbers are the starting point; the lab may propose changes with a screenshot (7). The WebGL renderer draws the moon as a world object on a UTC-locked orbit (7.10.4, 7.10.12), which the Canvas2D reference's camera-locked companion only approximates; for the moon the WebGL renderer is the contract.
6. **Boot timing.** The mock's boot is a timed simulation of about 8 s (6 s with `autoplay=1`); the real boot is paced by real requests and at least `--dur-boot-min`. The coach mark "That is the chain. Click it." (9.1) and the 24 achievements (9.9) are proposals awaiting the owner's approval.
7. **Specified but not mocked**: the phone moon parking as a header symbol, the queue wheel's slot advance on landing, the shell-ring-to-sky-orbit blend (7.10.12), the night lights, the Started, DoS and Unreachable node styles, compact relay for a second block within 3 s, the aperture's mouth ring, spring and white-hot entry (6.4 A), the focus and forced-colours styling.
8. **Localisation and fonts.** The mock ships latin and latin-ext subsets; Cyrillic and Vietnamese are needed if Atlas is translated.
9. **Trademark and naming.** "Atlas" is a working name for an ecosystem tool; confirm the wording of the trademark notice with Flux before launch.

Settled in v0.3 and no longer open: the relay fires in the order of the coinbase's own outputs (6.4 I); freshness is judged at 1.5x, 3x and 10x the source's cadence (6.5); the Globe Lab's world-space moon and the camera-locked companion are one object in two modes (7.10.12). Settled in v0.3.1: that object is a world object on a UTC-locked orbit in both modes, which the planet may hide (7.10.4).
